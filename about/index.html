Everyone’s cards on their own phone. The table on your TV.

Lamplight is a card table for game night. One of you starts a table, everyone else scans the code, and the cards are dealt straight to each phone. 31 games, from Go Fish to bridge. It’s free and runs in the browser, so there’s nothing to install.

Getting a game going

Open Lamplight on any phone, tablet or laptop and pick a game. A five-letter code comes up with a QR code under it. Everyone else scans it, or types the letters on their own phone.

Short of players? Put bots in the empty seats, on Easy, Normal or Hard. The lobby also has each game’s usual house rules, so you can play Hearts the way your family does.

If there’s a TV in the room, cast the table to it, or open it on a laptop in the middle. Everyone watches the tricks land there and keeps their own cards in their hand.

Some of the evenings it’s for

After dinner

Hearts round the kitchen table, with the game on the TV. Nobody has to shuffle or keep score.

Only three of you? A bot takes the fourth seat.

Poker night, three tables

More people came than fit one table, so it’s a tournament, run from a laptop. Players sign up with a code, each phone is told where to sit, and the blinds go up on a clock on the TV. When a table breaks, its players are sent to their new seats.

The same goes for Hearts, Euchre or anything else: Swiss rounds, round robins or a knockout.

Your bridge four, one of them across the country

Send them the invite link. They play from home, the other three from the living room.

For bridge players

Clubs can run duplicate, pairs or teams: every table plays the same boards, and each result is scored against the others when the round is done.

For learning, there’s practice. Choose what to work on, from openings to answering a weak two, and each deal is dealt for it. Your first call is checked against the system the bots play, SAYC or two over one, with the reason. Get it wrong and you can try again before the auction moves on.

Practice checks your first call only. After that, it’s an ordinary hand: bid it out and play it.

Good to know

Turn your phone sideways.
Most games are laid out for a phone on its side. Held upright, it will ask you to turn it.
Same Wi-Fi is quickest.
Friends somewhere else join with the invite link instead of the code. It works, a little slower.
If the host’s phone dies, the game goes on.
The phone that started the table runs the game. If it drops out, another player can take over where it left off.
Add it to your Home Screen.
Then it opens like an app, full screen, and starts even without a signal once you’ve used it.
