How it works

Seated in seconds.

  1. Start a table

    On any phone, tablet or laptop. Choose the game; set the house rules if you like.

  2. Everyone joins

    They scan the code on your screen, or type its five letters. Friends somewhere else get a link.

  3. Deal

    Your cards stay on your phone, for your eyes only. Tap to play, and the table keeps score.

Bring in the TV

The table on the big screen, the hands in yours.

Cast the game to your TV, or open it on any screen in the room. Everyone sees the cards in play, the pot and the score, while their own hand stays private on their phone.

  • Casts to a TV in a tap, or opens on a laptop or tablet in the middle of the table.
  • Bots fill empty seats, at Easy, Normal or Hard. Two of you can play a four-player game.
  • Nobody gets lost: whose turn it is, what was led and what the score is, always on show.
  • A phone dies? Play on. Someone else picks up the game where it left off.

Bridge practice

Bid, and see what the book says.

Pick a topic, from openings to answering a weak two. Each deal is made for it, your first call is checked, and you can try again before the auction goes on. Then play the hand out.

Tournaments

From four friends to a full room.

Run a tournament from a laptop: Swiss rounds, round robins and knockouts of any game, multi-table poker, and duplicate bridge. Players sign up with a code, and every phone is told where to sit for the next round.

  • The big screen shows the tables, the standings or the bracket, or follows the games live.
  • Scores add up by themselves, round after round, with byes and pairings handled.
  • A second device can stand by to take over if the director’s laptop goes quiet.
